How a lead moves through the qualification machine

Three ways a lead arrives. One qualifier that does not care which. Three ways it ends, and one way it goes round again. Every gate on this page is a door that can stay shut.

STAGE 01

Three front doors

The dividing line is not the campaign — it is whether a 24-hour WhatsApp window is open. That single fact decides whether we may write freely or must send pre-approved copy.

Click-to-WhatsApp ad They message us first, straight off the ad. Window open · free text allowed
Website or form lead They left details. We have to reach out. Window shut · template only
Old-lead reactivation Someone from an earlier list. Cold. Window shut · template only · cold
STAGE 02

Intake — stamp who this lead belongs to

The project is taken from the ad or the list the lead came from — never from what the person types. That stamp is what later keeps one project's answers out of another project's conversations.

STAGE 03

Suppression gate — may we contact this person at all?

An old lead is not a blank contact. Before a single message is scheduled we read their stage and labels in the CRM and compare against a list the sales team controls — changing it is a data edit, not a software release.

already owned by a salesperson already rejected already bought ever said stop invalid number
Any match ⇒ never knocked, ever
checked on every single message
STAGE 04

The send gate — one bouncer at one door

Every outbound message in the whole system leaves through one place, and passes four checks about the person, not the record — the same human can sit in our database three times, and one ledger covers all three. What comes back from WhatsApp (delivered, read, failed) is recorded against them.

Lane A · live

They are already talking to us

The window is open, so the bot replies in its own words immediately. Nothing to schedule, nothing to approve. Straight into qualification.

Lane B · knock

We have to knock and wait

Four knocks, then silence means silence. A knock has one job: earn a reply. Not a booking, not interest — any reply at all, because the reply is what opens the 24-hour window and lets the bot talk. "Who is this?" is a complete success.

day 0 · lifestyle day 3 · location day 10 · low density day 25 · visit invitation day 31 → dormant

Every ask is deliberately low-friction and free of pressure. Buttons request information — send the map, what's nearby — or pose one either-or question. Never a location or budget question on a cold knock: both can end a conversation before it starts.

One engine serves both cold old leads and people who went quiet mid-conversation — they are the same condition, so they share one fatigue counter. Two engines would double-message anyone in both.

STAGE 05

The qualifier — answers first, then asks

Source-agnostic: it does not know or care which door the lead came through. It picks up at the saved checklist position and never restarts the conversation. The checklist is a background objective, not a form — a buyer's question always gets answered before the next ask.

ASKED 1ST Purpose Never rejects · flags fit
ASKED 2ND Location Wide gate
ASKED 3RD Configuration Soft gate
ASKED LAST Budget Hard gate · earned
Budget is asked last, not first. It is earned by having been useful, not demanded up front. For a weekend-home product it is also the only hard filter left.
Location is a wide gate. Anywhere in or around Chennai passes, OMR included. It fails only on a different city, or on someone who finally rules out ECR — and the bot must answer that doubt before it may reject on it. "ECR is too far" is a question, not a decision.
Timeline is captured, never used to reject. Same for purpose — a slow buyer is still a buyer. But a buyer who wants this as a daily-commute family home rides on the card flagged, so the salesperson resets expectations on the call rather than at the site.
Answers come only from this project's knowledge base. Ranges, never exact figures. For Republic of Nature today, no price at all — every price question goes to a human.
Three different ways to ask each question, never a repeat. After three answers given with nothing back, a human is flagged — and the bot keeps answering meanwhile.
The bot books the site visit. It takes a day and a time and tells them it is booked — then that the team will call to confirm timing and share directions. Not a bare "confirmed": there is no calendar behind it, and a buyer standing at a gate on a Saturday is worse than never having booked. Tuesday is the team's day off; Monday afternoons only.
The site comes first, always. The Experience Centre at Express Avenue is offered only to someone worried about the distance — a first look during the week, with the real visit at the weekend. Volunteering it unprompted would quietly turn site visits into mall visits.
Never implies a natural private beach. The approved wording is a planned man-made beach and lagoon experience within the community.
stage 06 · four ways out
Terminal Qualified Budget and location clear, configuration fine or flagged. Sales gets a ping.
Loop-back — not an exit Goes silent Window shuts mid-checklist. Returns to the knock lane where it left off.
Terminal Dead Fails budget, or wants to buy in another city, or wants something we do not sell. Suppressed permanently — never touched again by anything.
Terminal for the bot Out of depth An objection, price pushback, a hard question with no good answer. The bot stops talking rather than improvising. These are disproportionately the real buyers.
└──► Silence returns to the knock lane mid-checklist, not to the beginning. Four knocks are all anyone ever gets. After day 31 of silence the lead goes dormant: the record is kept, and only a human may wake it, only for a genuinely new reason. The bot may never restart a journey by itself. That is the line between following up and harassing someone, and it is enforced in code rather than left to judgement.

The handoff — and what sales actually receives

A qualified lead becomes one message into the sales WhatsApp group, not a page someone has to go and look at. The full conversation is stored on our side for audit and for a later CRM push.

purpose location configuration budget timeline source what they asked about any day they volunteered

Then a salesperson calls and closes for the site visit. A site visit happening is the win — the bot's entire job is to make that call worth making.

Still open — decisions this chart cannot make
