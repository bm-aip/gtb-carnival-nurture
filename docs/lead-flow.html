The qualification machine

This bot replaces the presales team. Its job is to do what a presales caller does — qualify, filter, and hand sales only the leads worth their time. Sales set the bar. Sales own the knowledge. The bot enforces both, at scale, on WhatsApp, for free.

Two front doors
Primary · starting here

Click-to-WhatsApp ad

They message us first. That one fact deletes the hardest problem we had — the 24-hour free-text window opens on arrival, at no cost.

  • No template, no Meta gate — on first contact
  • Ad ID → brand, stamped exactly, on contact
  • Click ID captured → the flywheel below
  • Bot starts qualifying immediately

Careful: CTWA doesn't delete the template tax — it moves it. Go silent and the window shuts, and the second knock costs a template again. Meta's gate is waiting on the ghost path.

The bot does presales
Ask in this order. It is the inverse of what matters.

Sales agreed this bar, so sales can't disown it. "What's your budget?" is a brutal opening line to a stranger who just tapped an Instagram ad — presales people don't lead with it, they earn it. So the bot opens with the questions that cost them nothing, and spends the trust it earns on the one that decides everything.

1 Purpose never gates Home, or investment? One word to answer — and it reframes everything after it.
2 Location hard gate Where they want to buy. Hunting on OMR? Not a fit, whatever the budget.
3 Config soft gate 2BHK vs 3BHK doesn't reject — sales has tools. Only a plot or a shop does.
4 Budget hard gate The sharpest filter — asked last, because it's earned.
Purpose is a lens, not a filter

An end-user wants schools, commute, possession, EMI, which floor gets the light. An investor wants yield, appreciation, resale, exit — he does not care about the playground. Both are real buyers; neither gets rejected. But the bot has to know which before it opens its mouth. Same knowledge base, different half of it.

Timeline — asked, but never a gate

Every buyer says "soon", so it's the one slot people misreport. Gate on it and you reject honest buyers who happen to be six months out. The bot records it and hands it to sales as intelligence, not a verdict — "ready in 3 months" and "ready in 14" both reach a salesperson, who decides who to call first.

The bot answers their questions from that brand's brochures and FAQs — in ranges, never exact figures — to earn the right to ask its own. It warms the site visit. It does not book it. Sales closes that.
Four ways out

Qualified

Budget + location clear · config OK or flagged

Written to Sell.do — the durable record, where sales already live. Purpose, timeline and any config gap ride along as context.

Pinged to the WhatsApp group — the interrupt that gets someone moving today.

Sales calls. Sales closes for the site visit. They only ever touch leads that are already real.

Ghost

Started, then went silent · the biggest bucket

Clicked the ad, said hi, answered a question or two, vanished. The window shuts 24 hours later.

Not qualified. Not dead either — they clicked a property ad and started a conversation.

We knock again: one template, then two more spaced tries. If they come back, the bot picks up where it left off — it doesn't start over.

Because budget is asked last, most ghosts vanish before it — so we already know their purpose, location and config. Which writes the re-open message for us: "You were looking at a 3BHK in Vandalur — shall I send you the price range?" That's the budget question in disguise, and it's shaped exactly like the utility template Meta doesn't block.

Out of depth

Objection · price pushback · hard question

The bot stops talking and hands over, flagged. It does not improvise.

These are not the leftovers — these are the buyers. The ones who push back are the ones who care. This queue cannot be left unwatched.

Dead

Only budget or location can send you here

Budget too low · hunting in the wrong micro-market · wants something we flatly don't sell (a plot, a shop) · no intent · typed STOP · junk.

Suppressed permanently. Never messaged again, by anything, ever.

Includes the buyer who'd fit the other brand. By decision we don't cross-sell — leads stay in their lane.

Inside one bot turn — where the ring-fence lives

Two brands, one system. A RON agent must never reach an Elements document — a leaked cross-brand price is the kind of mistake a buyer screenshots. So the fence is a database filter, not an instruction to the model. Models drift; WHERE clauses don't.

1 · They ask "What's the price of the 3BHK?"
2 · Lock the brand Brand comes from the lead record — stamped from the ad they clicked. WHERE brand_id = lead.brand. Never from what they typed. No phrasing jumps the fence.
3 · Search that brand only The closest passages from that brand's brochures, floor plans and FAQ. Sales keep these current.
4 · Is it sure? Nothing solid comes back → it may not answer from its own general knowledge. That's where invented possession dates come from. It escalates instead.
5 · Answer, then advance Answers from the documents, in a range never an exact figure — then pushes the checklist forward.
6 · Log everything The reply is stored with the exact documents and versions that produced it.

Why step 6 matters more than it looks: one day a buyer will say "your bot told me ₹X." You'll pull that conversation and the exact document version that was live that day, and know in seconds whether they're right. In property, that isn't housekeeping — that's your defence.

The flywheel — your ad spend teaches itself

Click-to-WhatsApp hands us a click ID that traces the conversation back to the exact ad that produced it. Which means we can tell Meta "this one qualified" — not "this one clicked."

Meta shows the ad → they message us → bot qualifies them → we tell Meta: QUALIFIED → Meta hunts more like him ↺

Meta stops optimising for cheap clicks and starts hunting people who actually qualify. Same budget, better leads, every week, with nobody touching a dial.

Must verify before building Meta puts the click ID in its webhook. Wati sits in between, and whether Wati forwards that field to us is undocumented anywhere. One CTWA click, catch the payload, read it with our own eyes. If we don't capture the click ID at the moment of arrival, it is gone forever — and so is the flywheel.

Always on

Not steps in the flow — they apply at every point in it. And every one is enforced by the system, not by asking the bot nicely.

The brand fence A database filter, not an instruction. Cross-brand leakage is impossible, not unlikely.
Price = range only Enforced by what we load. Ranges go in; exact cost sheets stay out. What isn't in the knowledge base cannot be said.
No source, no answer The bot repeats what it found. It cannot fall back on what it "knows".
Never commits No discounts, no promises, no site-visit slot. It doesn't book — it hands over.
Retry ceiling Replaces today's loop, which retries a failed send every 5 minutes forever — the event ending is the only thing that ever stopped it.
Opt-out "STOP" honoured permanently, across both brands.
Fatigue cap A hard lifetime limit on how many times one person can be messaged.
Full audit Every answer stored with the documents that produced it.

What's still missing before a line of code gets written

Sales have agreed a bar. We don't yet have their numbers — and those three numbers are the bot. Everything else is plumbing around them.

  • Budget (hard gate) — what's the floor, per brand? Hard cutoff, or a band?
  • Location (hard gate) — which micro-markets count as a fit? Elements is at Vandalur — does Tambaram qualify? Where exactly do we draw the line?
  • Configuration (soft gate) — two things: what's sellable right now (this also goes into the knowledge base), and what counts as wildly off? What do we flatly not sell — plots? commercial? rentals? Only that second list rejects anyone.
  • Escalations — the same WhatsApp group, or its own? Whoever watches it is watching your best leads.
  • Sell.do — today we only read from it. Writing a qualified lead in needs API access we don't have. That's a request to someone outside the team, and those take days. Start it now.
